About

Machine learning across biomedical engineering, neuroscience, and real-world systems.

I am a PhD candidate in Computer Science at the Institute of Mathematics and Computer Sciences, University of São Paulo (ICMC-USP). My research investigates how relational inductive biases can improve learning from small, heterogeneous clinical datasets, with a current focus on population similarity graphs and graph neural networks for cognitive-status modeling in Alzheimer-related data.

My background combines Biomedical Engineering, computational neuroscience, and industry machine learning. I conducted fMRI research at the University of Bristol and later worked as a Junior Data Scientist at unico IDTech, developing and evaluating machine-learning systems under class imbalance, data scarcity, and production constraints.

Research

Relational learning for reliable AI in small, heterogeneous clinical datasets.

My doctoral research develops and evaluates Population Similarity Graph methods for heterogeneous clinical and sociodemographic data. I study when graph-based relational learning provides measurable advantages over models that treat individuals independently, while keeping validation, robustness, and inference for unseen individuals central to the experimental design.

Relational Learning & GNNs

Representing individuals as nodes in population-level graphs and learning from similarity relationships using graph neural networks and structured representations.

Graph Neural Networks Population Similarity Graphs Representation Learning

Reliable Small-Data ML

Designing leakage-controlled experiments for limited and imbalanced clinical datasets, with emphasis on nested cross-validation, stability, class-wise performance, and robust model comparison.

Nested Cross-Validation Robustness Imbalanced Learning

Inductive Graph Inference

Investigating inference protocols that allow new individuals to be connected to a learned population graph without rebuilding the entire training system.

Inductive Learning Generalization

Model Understanding

Using feature-importance, explanation, ablation, and error-analysis methods to understand what drives model predictions and where performance breaks down across cognitive-status classes.

Explainability Ablation Studies Error Analysis
