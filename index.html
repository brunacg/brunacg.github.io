About

Machine learning across biomedical engineering, neuroscience, and real-world systems.

My work sits at the intersection of graph-based learning, small-data methodology, and healthcare applications.

I am a PhD candidate at the Institute of Mathematics and Computer Sciences, University of São Paulo (ICMC-USP). My current research focuses on relational machine learning and graph neural networks for heterogeneous Alzheimer-related data, with particular interest in population similarity graphs and inductive inference for unseen individuals.

My background combines Biomedical Engineering, computational neuroscience, and industry machine learning. Current research

Population similarity graphs for heterogeneous clinical data.

My doctoral research asks whether relational inductive biases can help when participant-level datasets are small, heterogeneous, and moderately imbalanced — without sacrificing methodological rigor.

The central idea is to represent participants not only by their own features, but also through their similarity relationships to other participants. This creates a population graph in which graph neural networks can model individual information together with relational structure.

Evaluation

Cognitive-status prediction under leakage-controlled validation.

Inductive inference: new participants are connected to the learned population structure and evaluated without rebuilding the full training pipeline.
RQ1 · Relational signal

When does relational information help?

I compare graph-based learning with models that treat participants independently to identify when population structure provides a measurable advantage.

RQ2 · Generalization

How should graph models handle new participants?

I investigate inductive inference protocols that connect unseen individuals to a learned population structure without rebuilding the full system.

RQ3 · Reliability

How reliable are conclusions from small clinical datasets?

I use leakage-controlled nested validation, robustness analysis, class-specific evaluation, and ablation studies to separate promising patterns from unstable results.
